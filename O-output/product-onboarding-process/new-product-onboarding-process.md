# New Product Introduction Process

**Owner:** Nadav Leventer, New Business Development (NBD)
**Version:** 2.0, October 2026 (merges the NBD draft with Wendy's v1.1)

## Overview

**What it is.** One process for bringing any new product into Systematics, from first look to "selling, supported and owned by a business unit." We check the product first and sign with the vendor only after a Go at Gate 1. Small products move faster, but none skip a gate.

**Target: about 8 weeks from first look to launch, handover at day 90.**

**Two routes, same process.**

| | NBD route | BU route |
|---|---|---|
| Who brings the product | NBD | A business unit manager |
| **Sponsor** (vendor, business case, gates) | Nadav | The BU manager |
| **Gate 0 group** | Nadav, Roni, Gili | BU manager and BU management team |
| **Launch owner** | Gili | Chosen by the BU manager |
| **Technical lead** | Roni | The BU's technical lead |
| Owning BU | Chosen at intake, takes over at Gate 4 | The BU that brought it |

## Stages and Gates

| # | Stage | Weeks | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | First look | 0 to 1 | **G0** Worth a full look? |
| 2 | Evaluate | 1 to 4 | **G1** Go-to-build? Signature follows a Go |
| 3 | Sign and set up | 4 to 6 | feeds G2 |
| 4 | Enable | 4 to 7 | **G2** Ready to sell? |
| 5 | Launch and pilot | 7 to 8 | **G3** Launch +30 days |
| 6 | Learn and hand over | to day 90 | **G4** Handover |

Stages 3 and 4 run in parallel. Stopping at any gate is a valid result.

### 1. First look (weeks 0 to 1)
Desk research, first vendor call, partner terms and Israel territory, whether anyone already sells it here. Output: a half-page first look.

**G0, decided together by the Gate 0 group, equal say.** Checks: domain fit, growth priority it serves, customer value, why Systematics, growth-engine potential, priority against current work. Outcomes: move on, park with a date, or drop, with a one-line reason. No agreement means park until the next meeting.

### 2. Evaluate (weeks 1 to 4)
- **Intake:** what it is, capabilities, deployment and security, vendor maturity, launch owner, owning BU.
- **Market:** customer problem, buyers and users, target segments, competition in Israel, willingness to pay. **3 to 5 customer conversations before G1.**
- **Portfolio and company:** fit, gaps, services we can attach, margin and break-even, risks. **Existing vendor check:** does it sit on, compete with, or clash with a current vendor agreement? Each conflict gets an owner and a date.
- **Commercial design:** positioning, packaging (pilot, team, enterprise), sales motion, proposed price. **Finance and the owning BU set the price.**

**G1, Go-to-build?** Sponsor and owning BU manager. Outcomes: Go, Go with conditions (owner and date each), or Stop.

### 3. Sign and set up (weeks 4 to 6)
Vendor agreement, then in parallel: SAP Business One (item code, cost, price list, invoicing), Salesforce (catalogue, price book, quote template), a dummy order end to end, support tickets (category, routing, escalation to vendor), demo environment, delivery and SOW, web page, legal.

### 4. Enable (weeks 4 to 7)
Sales Brief and Marketing Brief, 3 pages max each. Enablement for sales, presales, marketing, support, services, customer success, finance and ops, **run inside the owning BU**. Each team proves readiness: a pitch to a peer, a live demo, or a resolved test ticket.

**G2, Ready to sell?** Every item is a yes, or the gap has an owner and a date:
- contract signed
- price in Salesforce
- dummy order runs
- teams pass enablement
- support can escalate a ticket
- pilot SOW ready
- marketing ready
- 5 to 10 target accounts named

### 5. Launch and pilot (weeks 7 to 8)
Internal announcement first (what it is, who sells it, how to get help), then customer outreach to named accounts, with a vendor co-announcement where it fits. Start 2 to 3 paid pilots. Weekly 15-minute launch huddle.

**G3, Launch +30 days.** Pipeline, first orders, pilots, support load, field feedback. Outcomes: continue, fix, or pause.

### 6. Learn and hand over (to day 90)
30/60/90 reviews against the business case. Convert pilots to subscriptions and turn the first into a case study. Fix price, message or training. One-page lessons note.

**G4, Handover.** NBD route: the BU manager accepts ownership, targets and budget, and NBD steps back. BU route: the BU manager closes the launch.

## Rules That Never Change
1. One launch plan per product, opening with a one-paragraph recommendation.
2. Vendor claims are unverified until customers or a pilot confirm them.
3. Finance and the owning BU set pricing. The launch owner proposes.
4. Enablement runs inside the owning BU. Sales and support are not centralized.
5. A paid pilot is standard. Only G1 can waive it, in writing.
6. Every task has one owner and a Thursday date.

## Who Does What
A = answers for it, R = does it, C = consulted, I = informed. In the BU route, Sponsor and owning BU manager are the same person.

| Activity | Sponsor | Tech lead | Launch owner | Owning BU | Finance / Ops |
|---|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| First look, G0 | A/R | R | R | | |
| Evaluate, business case | A/R | R | R | C | C |
| Pricing | C | C | R (proposes) | A/R | A/R |
| Vendor terms | A/R | C | C | C | C |
| Systems and operations | I | C | C | C | A/R |
| Briefs and enablement | I | R | R | A | R |
| Launch and pilots | A | C | R | R | C |
| Handover (G4) | A | C | R | R | C |

**Tracking:** a tracker in the launch plan lists each stage and gate with owner, date and status. A 15-minute weekly check reviews blockers and gate dates.
