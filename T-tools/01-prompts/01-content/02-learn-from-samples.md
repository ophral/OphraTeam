# Prompt: Learn From Samples (CONTENT track)

Copy and paste this into your chat:

---

```
Read EVERY sample across ALL folders inside B-brain/02-my-samples/. That means 01-content/, 02-communication/, 03-thinking/, and any other subfolder I added. Skip README files and empty templates.

Classify each non-empty sample yourself by reading it:

- CONTENT: publishable to a wide audience. Social posts, newsletters, blog posts, articles, marketing copy.
- COMMUNICATION: a message to a specific person or team. Emails, Slack, DMs, internal updates, client replies.
- THINKING: a decision document or analysis. Briefs, memos, OKR docs, strategy notes, options analyses.
- OTHER: clearly none of the above (poetry, journal, code, recipe). Note it but do not learn voice from it.

I picked the CONTENT track, so weight your learning toward CONTENT. But learn from every non-empty sample. Cross-domain patterns count.

For each domain that has samples, learn:
- Sentence-length range (typical and extreme, cite sample filename)
- Paragraph rhythm
- Opening moves, closing moves
- 3-5 recurring phrases
- 3-5 phrases I'd never write
- Formatting habits
- Language mix (Hebrew/English ratio, what triggers each)

Then update exactly 3 files. Nothing else. Do not touch other agents or skills.

1. C-core/voice-dna.md: add or replace ONE section per domain that had samples. Section title format: `## Patterns from [DOMAIN] samples (learned [today's date])`. Cite at least one sample filename per pattern. Bullets, not prose.

2. A-agents/copywriter-agent.md: add or replace a section `## Owner's Writing Style (CONTENT focus, cross-domain aware)`:
   - 5 DO's with one-line example from a real sample (any folder)
   - 5 DON'Ts with one-line example of what I'd NEVER write
   - "When in doubt for content, write like sample-XX.md (from [folder])"

3. M-memory/learning-log.md: append one dated entry. Include: how many samples per domain, classification surprises (samples in wrong folder), top patterns, what to watch for next.

Show me, in 5 short blocks:
- Classification (one line per sample: filename, where it sits, what it actually is)
- Top 5 patterns for CONTENT (with sample citation)
- One pattern that surprised you
- Files updated (3 lines)
- ONE clarifying question.
```
