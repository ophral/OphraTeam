# Skill: Budget Proposal

> Creates department budgets and investment cases that justify spend with clear expected returns.

## Trigger Words
"budget", "budget proposal", "investment case", "funding request", "department budget"

## Format

```markdown
# Budget Proposal: [Initiative/Department]

**Date:** [Date]
**Type:** Budget Proposal
**Requested by:** [Name, Title]
**Period:** [Fiscal year/quarter]
**Total ask:** [Amount]
**Decision needed by:** [Date]

---

## One-Line Ask

[Single sentence: what you need and what it delivers. Example: "$200K for 3 engineers to ship feature X, projected to add $1.2M ARR by Q4.]

---

## Business Case

**Problem:** [What happens if we don't fund this?]
**Opportunity:** [What becomes possible if we do?]
**Evidence:** [Data, benchmarks, or precedent supporting the case]
```

## Quality Checks

- [ ] Total ask is clear upfront (no burying the number)
- [ ] Return projections are realistic with stated assumptions
- [ ] Alternatives include "do nothing" with honest consequences
- [ ] Milestones have kill criteria (not just progress markers)
- [ ] Line items are specific enough to hold accountable

---

## Top-1% Knowledge

What separates a great practitioner of this format from an average one. Use these as the quality bar.

- **The 3-Scenario Frame.** Conservative, expected, aggressive. Force the reviewer to engage with assumptions, not just numbers.
- **ROI by category, not aggregate.** "Marketing ROI is 3x" hides bad channels. Break it down.
- **The Counterfactual.** What happens if we DON'T spend this. Bare opportunity cost beats abstract benefit.
- **Phasing over lump sum.** "Start with $100K, evaluate at Q2, expand to $400K if hitting metrics" beats "give us $500K now."
- **Anchor to a comparable.** "Industry average for this stage is X." Anchoring works.

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The predictable failure modes for this format. Check against these before shipping.

- Requesting without justifying. Numbers without narrative get cut first.
- No success metrics. "We'll spend $200K on growth" without "and here's how we measure it."
- Padding for negotiation. Reviewers can see padding. It hurts credibility.
- Ignoring the timing of cash. Budget approved doesn't mean cash available.
- No alternative considered. Plan A without Plan B suggests you didn't think hard enough.
