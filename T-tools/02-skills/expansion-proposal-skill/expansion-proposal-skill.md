# Skill: Expansion Proposal

> Creates upsell and cross-sell proposals that start with customer value, not your revenue targets.

## Trigger Words
"expansion", "upsell", "cross-sell", "upgrade proposal", "growth proposal"

## Format

```markdown

**Date:** [Date]
**Type:** Expansion Proposal
**Customer:** [Company]
**Current plan:** [What they have now]
**Proposed:** [What we're suggesting]
**Prepared by:** [CSM name]

---

[2-3 sentences: what triggered this proposal. A milestone they hit, a problem they mentioned, usage growth, a business change. Must be rooted in THEIR context, not your quota.]
```

## Quality Checks

- [ ] Proposal is triggered by customer context (not internal targets)
- [ ] Value is projected using their metrics and KPIs
- [ ] Implementation effort is honest (not minimized to close deal)
- [ ] Alternatives give real choice (not just "take it or leave it")
- [ ] Customer can read this and feel served, not sold to

---

## Top-1% Knowledge

What separates a great practitioner of this format from an average one. Use these as the quality bar.

- **Value-Stack Frame.** Start with their realized value to date. Then project the next tier of value. Anchor the expansion in their own results.
- **The Land-and-Expand Narrative.** "You started with X seats. Here's the natural next phase." Make expansion feel inevitable, not asked-for.
- **Mutual Success Plan.** Co-author the next 90 days with explicit milestones. Buyers approve plans they helped write.
- **Pricing Anchoring.** Lead with the higher tier. Make the chosen tier feel reasonable by comparison.
- **The Sponsor Test.** Before sending the proposal, your sponsor should be able to pitch it internally. If they can't, the proposal isn't ready.

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The predictable failure modes for this format. Check against these before shipping.

- Pitching new features when they need new outcomes.
- No connection to current realized value. "Here's more" without "because the first thing worked."
- Single contact. Champion approves. Their boss vetoes. Multi-thread.
- Discount-as-strategy. Trains buyers to push every renewal.
- No risk reversal. Money-back, success-criteria-tied, or graceful-exit clauses lower the no.
