# Skill: Executive Summary

> Creates one-pagers that give leadership everything they need to decide, in two minutes or less.

## Trigger Words
"executive summary", "one-pager", "leadership brief", "decision doc", "BLUF"

## Format

```markdown
# Executive Summary: [Topic]

**Date:** [Date]
**Type:** Executive Summary
**Author:** [Name]
**Decision needed:** [Yes/No]
**Time to read:** [1-2 minutes]

---

## Bottom Line

[2-3 sentences: the conclusion, recommendation, or key finding. Put the answer first.]

---

## Context

[3-4 sentences maximum: what the reader needs to know to understand the bottom line. No history lessons.]

---

## Key Data

| Metric | Value | So What |
|--------|-------|---------|
| [Metric] | [Number] | [Why it matters] |
| [Metric] | [Number] | [Why it matters] |
| [Metric] | [Number] | [Why it matters] |

---

## Options (if decision needed)

| Option | Pros | Cons | Cost |
|--------|------|------|------|
| [Option A] | [Key benefit] | [Key risk] | [Cost/effort] |
| [Option B] | [Key benefit] | [Key risk] | [Cost/effort] |

**Recommendation:** [Which and why, in one sentence]

---

## Next Steps

- [ ] [Action, owner, by when]
- [ ] [Action, owner, by when]
- [ ] [Action, owner, by when]
```

## Quality Checks

- [ ] Readable in under 2 minutes
- [ ] Bottom line is actually at the top (not buried)
- [ ] No unnecessary context or background
- [ ] Data has "so what" column (numbers alone don't inform)
- [ ] Next steps have owners and dates

---

## Top-1% Knowledge

What separates a great practitioner of this format from an average one. Use these as the quality bar.

- **BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front).** Military and McKinsey standard. First sentence is the recommendation.
- **The 30/60/90 rule.** Executive reads the first 30 seconds, decides if to keep going. First 60 if intrigued. First 90 if you've earned it.
- **Pyramid Principle (Minto).** Recommendation. Three supporting reasons. Evidence per reason. Never the reverse.
- **The Single-Sentence Test.** If you can't summarize the recommendation in one sentence, you don't have one yet.
- **Risks at the bottom, not the top.** Lead with the recommendation. End with the considered risks. Inverted structure signals weak conviction.

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The predictable failure modes for this format. Check against these before shipping.

- Burying the recommendation. Page 3 of 5 is too late.
- Background before recommendation. Most executive readers won't scroll past background.
- No recommendation, only options. Your job is to recommend, not just present.
- Hedge words throughout. "Could," "might," "potentially." Each one drains conviction.
- Length creep. "Executive summary" that's three pages defeats its own definition.
