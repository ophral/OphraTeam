# Skill: Competitive Analysis

> Creates competitor breakdown documents that inform strategy without inducing paranoia.

## Trigger Words
"competitive analysis", "competitor", "market landscape", "competitive intel", "competitor comparison"

## Quality Checks

- [ ] Analysis answers a specific strategic question (not just "who's out there")
- [ ] Competitor strengths are acknowledged honestly (no dismissing)
- [ ] Data has sources or is marked as estimates
- [ ] Implications lead to actions (not just awareness)
- [ ] Freshness date included (competitive intel expires)

---

## Top-1% Knowledge

What separates a great practitioner of this format from an average one. Use these as the quality bar.

- **Porter's Five Forces.** Rivalry, supplier power, buyer power, substitutes, new entrants. Structural analysis beats spot checks.
- **Hamilton Helmer's 7 Powers.** Which competitors have which power? Scale, network, counter-positioning, switching costs, branding, cornered resource, process.
- **Positioning Map (2x2s).** Pick the two dimensions buyers actually care about. Plot competitors. Find the empty quadrant.
- **The Win/Loss Pattern.** Talk to deals you won and lost. The pattern beats the punditry.
- **The Job To Be Done lens.** Sometimes the real competitor isn't a product. It's the spreadsheet, the status quo, the consultant.

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The predictable failure modes for this format. Check against these before shipping.

- Feature comparison tables that ignore positioning. Features can be matched. Position cannot.
- Focusing on the loudest competitor. Sometimes the quiet one is the threat.
- Underestimating substitutes and overestimating direct rivals.
- No "why we win, why we lose." Without these, you're describing, not analyzing.
- Snapshot analysis. Markets move. Refresh quarterly minimum.
