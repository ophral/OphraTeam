# Skill: Employee Communication

> Creates clear internal announcements and change communications that inform without alarming.

## Trigger Words
"internal comm", "announcement", "all-hands", "org change", "company update"

## Format

```markdown
# [Communication Title]

**Date:** [Date]
**Type:** Internal Communication
**Audience:** [All employees / Department / Leadership / Managers only]
**Urgency:** [FYI / Action Required / Time-Sensitive]
**Sent via:** [Email / Slack / All-hands / Town hall]

---

## TL;DR

[One sentence. If people read nothing else, this is it.]

---

## What's Changing

[2-4 sentences: what is happening, in plain language. No corporate jargon.]

---

## Why

[2-3 sentences: the honest reason. People can smell spin. Be direct.]

---

## What This Means for You

[Specific impact by audience. What changes in their day-to-day? What stays the same?]

---

## Timeline

| Date | What Happens |
|------|--------------|
| [Date] | [Event/milestone] |
| [Date] | [Event/milestone] |
| [Date] | [Event/milestone] |

---

## Questions?

[Where to go, who to ask, when the next Q&A is. Make it easy.]
```

## Quality Checks

- [ ] TL;DR actually summarizes everything
- [ ] No passive voice hiding accountability ("it was decided" vs "we decided")
- [ ] Impact section is specific, not vague reassurance
- [ ] Timeline has concrete dates (not "coming weeks")
- [ ] Tone matches severity (don't be cheerful about layoffs)

---

## Top-1% Knowledge

What separates a great practitioner of this format from an average one. Use these as the quality bar.

- **The Bad-News Sandwich antipattern.** Don't sandwich. Be direct, then supportive. People resent fake softening.
- **SBI (Situation-Behavior-Impact).** Anchor in observable behavior, not personality. "In the meeting yesterday (S), you spoke over Sarah three times (B), which shut down her contribution (I)."
- **The 24-Hour Rule.** For sensitive comms, draft, sleep, redraft. The morning version is always better.
- **Repetition discipline.** Important messages land on the 7th repetition, not the 1st.
- **Channel Match.** All-hands for direction. 1:1 for performance. Slack for logistics. Mismatched channels signal poor judgment.

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The predictable failure modes for this format. Check against these before shipping.

- Corporate-speak for emotional topics. "We are reorganizing" instead of "I'm changing your manager."
- Burying the lede in concerning emails. People assume the worst when leaders bury news.
- No FAQ for big announcements. The questions you don't answer fill with rumor.
- Treating broadcast as relationship. "I sent the email" doesn't equal "we talked."
- Ignoring the second-order audience. The message to the team gets forwarded. Write for the forward, not just the original.
