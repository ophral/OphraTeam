# Skill: Change Management

> Creates rollout plans and communication strategies for organizational changes that actually stick.

## Trigger Words
"change management", "rollout plan", "transition plan", "org change", "process change"

## Quality Checks

- [ ] "Why" is honest and compelling (not corporate fluff)
- [ ] Resistance is anticipated respectfully (not dismissed)
- [ ] Pilot has clear success criteria before broader rollout
- [ ] Communication cascades in the right order (leaders first)
- [ ] Rollback plan exists (shows confidence, not weakness)

## Top-1% Knowledge

What separates a great practitioner of this format from an average one. Use these as the quality bar.

- **Kotter's 8-step model.** Sense of urgency, guiding coalition, vision, communicate, empower, short-term wins, build on the change, anchor in culture.
- **The Change Curve (Kübler-Ross adapted).** Shock, denial, frustration, depression, experimentation, decision, integration. People go through this whether you address it or not.
- **SCARF (Status, Certainty, Autonomy, Relatedness, Fairness).** Threats to any of these trigger resistance. Address them explicitly.
- **The Stakeholder Map.** Who's a champion, who's a blocker, who's neutral. Plan messaging per group, not one-size-fits-all.
- **Communicate 7x.** Each message needs to land seven times before it's heard. Repetition isn't redundancy.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The predictable failure modes for this format. Check against these before shipping.

- One-and-done announcement. The change is announced. Resistance grows in the silence after.
- No "what's in it for them." The change benefits the company. Why should each affected person care?
- Treating resistance as obstruction. Resistance is signal. Resistance is information about gaps in your plan.
- Ignoring the middle managers. Top leadership commits. Front-line knows. Middle layer kills it.
- Declaring victory too early. Year 2 is when changes actually take hold or revert.
