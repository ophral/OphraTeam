# Skill: Board Report

> Creates board meeting narratives and financial summaries that respect directors' time and drive decisions.

## Trigger Words
"board report", "board meeting", "board deck", "directors update", "board narrative"

## Format

```markdown
# Board Report: [Period]

**Date:** [Date]
**Type:** Board Meeting Narrative
**Prepared by:** [Name, Title]
**Meeting date:** [Date]
**Confidentiality:** Board Only

---

## Executive Summary

- [Key highlight: biggest win or milestone this period]
- [Key concern: most important risk or challenge]
- [Key ask: what the board needs to decide or approve]

---

## Financial Highlights

| Metric | Target | Actual | vs. Target | vs. Last Period |
|--------|--------|--------|------------|-----------------|
| Revenue | [Amount] | [Amount] | [+/-  %] | [+/- %] |
| Gross Margin | [%] | [%] | [+/- pp] | [+/- pp] |
| Burn Rate | [Amount] | [Amount] | [+/- %] | [+/- %] |
| Runway | [Months] | [Months] | [+/-] | [+/-] |
| [Key Metric] | [Target] | [Actual] | [Variance] | [Trend] |

**Narrative:** [2-3 sentences explaining the numbers. What drove the results? Any one-time items?]

---

## Key Decisions Needed

### Decision 1: [Title]

**Context:** [2-3 sentences]
**Options:**
- Option A: [Description] / Impact: [What happens] / Risk: [Downside]
- Option B: [Description] / Impact: [What happens] / Risk: [Downside]
- Option C: [Description] / Impact: [What happens] / Risk: [Downside]

**Management recommendation:** [Which option and why]

## Looking Ahead

**Next quarter priorities:**
1. [Priority with expected outcome]
2. [Priority with expected outcome]
3. [Priority with expected outcome]

**Board action items from this meeting:**
- [ ] [Action, owner, deadline]
- [ ] [Action, owner, deadline]
```

## Quality Checks

- [ ] Numbers are verified and internally consistent
- [ ] Narrative matches the data (no spin)
- [ ] Decisions have clear options with tradeoffs
- [ ] Risks have named owners
- [ ] Executive summary is useful standalone (board members read this first)

---

## Top-1% Knowledge

What separates a great practitioner of this format from an average one. Use these as the quality bar.

- **BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front).** First slide is the decision needed. Everything else supports.
- **Reforge / Brian Balfour's metric narrative.** Don't just report numbers. Explain what drove them, what surprised you, what you're doing about it.
- **Bezos six-pager.** Replace slides with prose. Forces clarity, kills decoration.
- **The three categories.** Wins, concerns, asks. If a slide doesn't fit one, cut it.
- **Pre-read discipline.** Send the report 48 hours before the meeting. Use meeting time for decisions, not narration.

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The predictable failure modes for this format. Check against these before shipping.

- Reporting what happened without why it happened.
- Burying the ask. Boards want to know what they're deciding by slide 3, not slide 12.
- Vanity metrics. Total registrations, page views, GMV. Boards want the metric that converts to cash.
- No risks. A report without risks signals lack of awareness, not absence of risk.
- Reading the slides at the meeting. The board read them already. Use the time for discussion.
