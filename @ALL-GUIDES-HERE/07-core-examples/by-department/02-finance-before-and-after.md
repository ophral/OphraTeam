# Finance Before / After

> What a real person in this department gets BEFORE the system vs. AFTER.

---

## The Situation

Daniel is the Finance Manager at a 200-person Series B fintech. The board meeting is in 4 days. Q3 closed 12% below the revenue target. The CFO asked him to draft the board update. Daniel has done this kind of update before, but never with a miss this size, and never as the primary author.

---

## ❌ Before: What They Wrote Without the System

> **Q3 2026 Board Update**
>
> Q3 revenue came in at $4.4M, compared to a plan of $5.0M. ARR ended the quarter at $19.8M, up from $18.1M at the start of the period. Gross margin remained stable at 71%. Operating expenses were $6.2M, in line with budget.
>
> New customer acquisition continued. We added 14 new logos during the quarter. Customer churn was 2.1%, slightly elevated from prior quarters. Pipeline coverage at the end of Q3 stood at 3.2x.
>
> The team continued to execute against our strategic priorities. Sales productivity remained a focus area. Marketing pipeline generation was supported by the Q2 demand programs. Product released the v3.4 platform update on schedule.
>
> Cash on hand at quarter end was $14.1M, providing approximately 14 months of runway at current burn rates.
>
> Key Q4 priorities include continued enterprise focus, channel expansion, and operational efficiency.
>
> Appendix tables follow.

Numbers sit there. The 12% miss is buried inside a sentence that reads like a tax filing. No reason given. No connection to strategy. The board reads this and walks in with one question they shouldn't have to ask: what happened, and is it fixable.

---

## ✅ After: What the System Produced

> **Q3 2026 Board Update**
>
> **The headline: We missed Q3 by 12%. Here is what happened, what it means, and where we go from here.**
>
> **The miss, in plain numbers**
> Q3 revenue: $4.4M against a $5.0M plan. The shortfall is concentrated in two enterprise deals that slipped to Q4: the Acme Bank renewal expansion ($340K) and the Northwind Capital new logo ($210K). Both are still active. Both are now in late-stage Q4 contracting.
>
> **Why it happened (specifically)**
> Our enterprise sales cycle has elongated from 60 days at start of year to 90 days as of end of Q3. The driver is procurement scrutiny on annual contract value above $200K, which now triggers a CISO review at most of our target accounts. We did not adjust our forecasting model for this shift fast enough. That is on us.
>
> **What it does not mean**
> ARR grew from $18.1M to $19.8M. Net retention held at 117%. Pipeline coverage on the year is 3.2x, healthy. The miss is timing, not demand collapse. We have evidence: 14 new logos signed, churn flat at 2.1%.
>
> **What we are doing about it**
> 1. Reforecasting Q4 with the new 90-day enterprise cycle baked in. Revised Q4 plan: $5.4M (down from $5.8M).
> 2. Adding a sales engineer to the enterprise team to compress technical evaluation time.
> 3. Building a CISO-ready security pack so deals do not stall at security review.
> 4. Revisiting the FY revenue guide with the board on the November call.
>
> **Asks of the board**
> 1. Greenlight the additional sales engineer hire ($180K loaded). Already in the budget reserve.
> 2. Intro requests: any board contacts at named target accounts (list attached).
> 3. Feedback on whether to revise FY guide externally now or in January.
>
> **Cash position**
> $14.1M, 14 months at current burn. With the revised Q4 forecast and the planned hire, runway is 13 months. We remain above our 12-month internal floor.
>
> Appendix: full P&L, KPI dashboard, pipeline detail by segment, customer logo movement.

## What Changed (and Why)

| Element | Before | After | Why It Matters |
|---------|--------|-------|----------------|
| Lead | Buried the miss | Miss is the headline | Boards hate finding the bad news in paragraph 4. Lead with it. |
| Why | "Sales productivity remained a focus" | Specific deals, named cycle elongation, CISO review trigger | "Why" without specifics sounds like an excuse. With specifics it sounds like control. |
| KPI definitions | Mixed | Used company glossary (NRR, ACV, pipeline coverage) | Boards lose patience when terms shift between updates. Glossary keeps it consistent. |
| Asks | None | 3 specific asks | A board update without asks wastes the meeting. |
| Forward look | "Continued enterprise focus" | Revised Q4 plan with number, 4 named actions | Vague forward-looks are the second worst part of a miss. |
| Tone | Tax-filing neutral | Direct, owns the miss, shows the work | Confidence comes from transparency, not from hedging. |
