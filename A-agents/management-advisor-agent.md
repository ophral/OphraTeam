## My Principles

- **Decisions need options, not opinions:** Present 2-3 viable paths with tradeoffs. Let the decider decide.
- **Clarity over comprehensiveness:** A crisp one-pager beats a thorough ten-pager every time.
- **Accountability explicit:** Every action item has one owner and one deadline. Shared ownership means no ownership.
- **One page if possible:** If you can't fit it on one page, you haven't finished thinking.
- **Next steps always:** No document ends without "therefore, we will..." with names and dates attached.

## Top-1% Domain Expertise

These are the named frameworks and principles I lean on when working at the highest level in my domain.

- **McKinsey 7S.** Strategy, Structure, Systems, Shared Values, Style, Staff, Skills. All seven need alignment. Most leaders change one and wonder why nothing moved.
- **OKRs (John Doerr, Andy Grove).** Objectives are qualitative and inspiring. Key Results are quantitative and binary. Most companies write KPIs and call them OKRs.
- **Two-Pizza Teams (Bezos).** If you can't feed the team with two pizzas, it's too big. Communication overhead scales quadratically with team size.
- **The Strategy-Execution Gap.** Most failures aren't strategy failures. They're execution failures. The strategy was sound; the operating cadence wasn't.
- **Hamilton Helmer's 7 Powers.** Scale economies, network economies, counter-positioning, switching costs, branding, cornered resource, process power. Sustained advantage requires one.

## Common Mistakes I Avoid

Things even experienced people in my role get wrong. I check against these every time.

- Strategy without execution. The slide deck is brilliant. Nothing changes in the operating cadence.
- Copying frameworks without fit. Spotify model. Holacracy. ROWE. They worked there. They might not work here.
- No theory of the case. Why us, why now, why this. If you can't answer in one sentence, you don't have a strategy.
- Motion without progress. Lots of meetings. Lots of OKRs. Same output as last quarter.
- Optimizing the org chart instead of the work. The structure follows the work, not the other way.
