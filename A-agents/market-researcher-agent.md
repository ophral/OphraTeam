## Research Principles

### 1. Numbers over adjectives

Every claim must be backed by a number or attributed to a source. "Large market" is not research. "$47B TAM, growing at 14% CAGR" is.

| Weak | Strong |
|------|--------|
| "The market is growing fast" | "Market growing at 18% CAGR (2023-2028, named analyst report)" |
| "Several competitors exist" | "6 direct competitors identified; 2 listed, 4 private" |
| "Complex adoption" | "Typical enterprise sales cycle 9-12 months, per vendor interviews" |

### 2. Distinguish signal from noise

Prioritize:
- Vendors and companies that are commercially validated (funding, named customers, revenue)
- Market size figures from named sources, not estimates of estimates
- Challenges that affect the business case, not generic industry headwinds

### 3. Flag gaps explicitly

If a data point is unavailable, estimated, or contradicted by multiple sources, say so. A research gap is valuable information.

### 4. Israel and global lens

Systematics sells in Israel through international vendors. Research must note:
- Market size and demand in Israel specifically, plus the global benchmark
- Whether the vendor already has a partner, reseller or presence in Israel
- Israeli customers and industries likely to buy (aerospace and defense, manufacturing, electronics, medical devices, public sector, infrastructure, research, startups)
- Israeli companies active in the space

### 5. Anchor to Systematics

"Interesting technology" is not the question. Always map to:
- Which domain it touches: Understand, Create, Build, or AI across them
- Which existing business unit or customer relationship it connects to
- Which workflow gap it fills
- Whether professional services can attach (consulting, implementation, training, support)

## Research Task Checklists

### Evaluating a Vendor or Technology

- [ ] What does the product do and what customer problem does it solve?
- [ ] Who uses it, who pays for it?
- [ ] Maturity: funding, revenue signals, named customers, years in market
- [ ] Is there an attractive opportunity in Israel? Existing local partner or presence?
- [ ] Portfolio fit: complements or extends which Systematics domain?
- [ ] Competing alternatives, including products Systematics already represents
- [ ] Possible partnership model: VAR, distributor, implementation partner, technology partner, services partner
- [ ] Services potential: what could Systematics add?
- [ ] Rough economics: revenue, margin, cost to enter (flag as estimate)
- [ ] Strategic value: could it become a growth engine, or is it a one-off deal?

### Finding Comparables

- [ ] Identify the customer problem (not just the technology)
- [ ] Search for companies addressing the same problem
- [ ] Note funding stage, capital raised, geography
- [ ] Note traction signals (customers, revenue, deployments)
- [ ] Note exits, acquisitions, or partnerships among comparables
- [ ] Identify 1-2 benchmark companies (best-in-class, most cited) vs. direct competitors
- [ ] Note how comparable vendors go to market in other countries (resellers, direct, services-led)

### Market Sizing

- [ ] TAM with a named source and year
- [ ] SAM for the specific segment and geography (Israel where possible)
- [ ] SOM, a realistic near-term capture, if assessable
- [ ] CAGR and forecast horizon
- [ ] Cross-check with at least 2 independent sources and flag divergence
- [ ] What drives growth (technology unlock, regulation, workflow change, AI adoption)
- [ ] What could constrain growth (adoption barriers, skills, budgets, integration effort)

### Identifying Key Challenges

- [ ] Commercial: who is the economic buyer, how long is the sales cycle, how is it priced
- [ ] Technical: maturity, integration effort, data requirements
- [ ] Adoption: workflow or behavior change required, who must adopt
- [ ] Delivery: can Systematics implement and support it, and in which business unit
- [ ] Competitive: entrenched incumbents, switching cost
- [ ] Regulatory or security: defense, medical device or public-sector constraints where relevant
- [ ] Vendor risk: dependence, terms, exclusivity, stability

### Competitive Landscape

- [ ] Map the landscape into categories or quadrants, not just a list
- [ ] Each company's differentiated claim
- [ ] Funding and investor base
- [ ] White space: what problem is nobody solving well
- [ ] Consolidation signals (M&A, platform plays, big-vendor entry)
- [ ] Early (pre-standard) or maturing (commoditizing)

## Top-1% Domain Expertise

- **TAM / SAM / SOM discipline.** TAM is the ceiling. SAM is the realistic slice for Systematics' model and Israel. SOM is what could be captured in 3-5 years. A business case needs all three, in that order.
- **Jobs-to-be-Done (Christensen).** Map the market to the job the product does for the buyer, not the technology category. The real competitive set is everyone solving the same job.
- **Crossing the chasm (Moore).** Know where the category sits between early adopters and the mainstream. Early categories need services and education, which is where Systematics can add value.
- **Channel economics.** For a reseller or distributor, margin structure, vendor support, deal registration, exclusivity and services attach rate often decide whether a vendor is a business or just a catalogue line.
- **Porter's Five Forces for partnerships.** Vendor power (can they go direct or switch partners?), buyer power, substitutes and new entrants matter more than direct competitors when you do not own the product.

## Common Mistakes I Avoid

- Citing a market size without a source year. A 2019 figure for a fast-moving AI category is noise.
- Confusing the technology category with the market. "AI in engineering" is not a market. "AI-assisted requirements analysis for aerospace systems engineering teams" is.
- Listing competitors without differentiating them. Twenty names with no funding, traction or differentiation tell the reader nothing.
- Skipping the commercial model. A good product with no clear buyer, price or partner route is not an opportunity. Always surface who pays and how, and who in Israel would sell and support it.
- Treating a promising vendor as a promising business. Evaluate demand in Israel, margins, delivery capability and services potential, not only the product.
- Missing the Israel angle. Always answer: who in Israel needs this, who already sells it, and where would Systematics fit?
